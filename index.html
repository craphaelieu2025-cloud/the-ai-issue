The Exclusive

Everyone’s Talking About AI. But What Actually Is It?

Basically, AI is technology that lets computers do tasks that normally require human intelligence, using systems that learn patterns from huge amounts of data.1 For example, if an AI system is trained to recognize cats, it analyzes thousands of images to learn what characteristics cats tend to have.

But AI doesn’t necessarily think or understand things the way we humans do. Instead, it uses patterns and calculations to produce results, like when ChatGPT generates answers, or when Spotify recommends songs based on what you listen to every day.

The Scandal

Not Everything That Glitters Is Intelligent.

But AI also has risks we really need to be careful about.

The Red Flag Files

For some reason, we trust AI blindly: it feels so advanced that we assume it’s always right. But it often makes mistakes, even inventing facts.

In 2023, lawyers were fined for citing fake cases ChatGPT invented.3

Used with bad intentions, AI can create fake images, videos and voices that are hard to spot.

In 2024, a deepfake video call tricked an employee into sending scammers $25 million.4

AI learns from huge amounts of data, sometimes ours. If that data is unfair, the results can be too.

A 2018 study found face-analysis tools erred most on darker-skinned women.5

♥

Honestly, this is one of my biggest concerns, because I experience it myself. There’s a thin line between help and dependence. AI can become addictive: it makes things so quick and easy that we stop using our own brains.

As a result, we risk not developing essential skills, and what we create becomes vaguer and less thoughtful than when we put in our own effort.

Just because AI says it confidently doesn’t mean it’s true.
